About Me

Ecological Economist Exploring the Hidden Costs of Global Food Systems

I am a PhD candidate in economics at Northwest A&F University. My research is in ecological economics, where the central question is not only how land is used but what happens to the living systems within it.

Soil biological communities, nutrient cycles, and landscape-level ecosystem functioning underpin global agricultural productivity yet remain outside every existing trade and environmental accounting framework. I study how agricultural production generates costs to these systems, how those costs are embodied in trade flows and displaced across borders, and how consumption-based accounting can be extended to make the belowground dimension of global food systems visible and governable.

Four Pillars of My Research

The conviction linking my work: the functional biology of landscapes is foundational to food systems and systematically undervalued in economic governance.

Land Ecological Security

Mapping and modelling the integrity of landscapes that underpin agricultural systems across borders.

Belowground Multifunctionality

Making visible the soil biological communities and nutrient cycles absent from current trade accounting.

Consumption-based Accounting

Extending environmental accounts to capture belowground costs embodied in agricultural trade flows.

Equity of Burden Displacement

Examining who bears the ecological cost when consumption in one place degrades land in another.
