About Me

Ecological Economist Exploring the Hidden Costs of Global Food Systems

I'm a PhD candidate in Agricultural Economics and Management at Northwest A&F University, working at the intersection of ecological economics, trade, and planetary boundaries. My research asks a simple question with a complicated answer: when a country imports food, what happens to the land, soil, and ecosystems that produced it, and who ultimately bears that cost?

Soil biological communities, nutrient cycles, and landscape-level ecosystem functioning underpin global agricultural productivity, yet sit outside almost every trade and environmental accounting framework in use today. I combine multi-regional input–output modelling, spatial analysis, and machine learning to trace ecosystem pressures, including belowground ecosystem multifunctionality and land degradation, from where food is grown to where it's consumed, and to test how those pressures shift under climate change and trade equity.

Agricultural trade externalizes belowground ecosystem loss to climate-stressed exporting nations

Nihal Ahmed, Nisar Ahmed Khan, Józef Ober, Jianjun Huai

International Journal of Sustainable Development & World Ecology

DOI: 10.1080/13504509.2026.2726387

View Article
Abstract

International agricultural trade redistributes environmental burdens across borders, yet one cost remains unquantified: the loss of belowground ecosystem multifunctionality (BEMF) in producing nations, and the degree to which climate warming amplifies it. We provide the first global quantification of trade-embodied BEMF loss, coupling 15 soil biological and biogeochemical indicators with the FABIO multi-regional input-output framework (155 matched exporting nations, 123 commodity sectors) and CMIP6 projections under three emissions scenarios to 2100. International trade embodies 14.7% of global cropland BEMF production. Thermally stressed exporters sustain 37% lower per-hectare BEMF than climatically stable exporters, yet direct a larger share of their production into trade, so supply chains draw disproportionately on the most ecologically depleted regions. Export-driven hotspots concentrate in Brazil, Sudan, India, and Eswatini, with the Brazil-to-China soybean corridor the single largest transfer. We argue for integrating belowground indicators into consumption-based accounting and applying a Climate Exacerbation Factor within climate-adjusted supply chain governance.

Deep learning approaches for synthesizing Land Ecological Security pathways in global land-use scenarios

Nihal Ahmed, Nisar Ahmed Khan, Józef Ober, Muhammad Farrukh Shahzad

Resources, Environment and Sustainability

DOI: 10.1016/j.resenv.2026.100345

View Article
Abstract

Land-use scenario projections are essential tools for sustainable development planning, but conventional integrated assessment model (IAM) approaches face computational constraints and limited scenario diversity across land degradation risk categories. We present a deep-learning methodology for categorizing and synthesizing Land Ecological Security (LES) trajectories using artificial scenario generation based on SDG 15.3.1 degradation indicators. Examining 1,202 land-use projections from the CMIP6/ScenarioMIP LUH2 database, we trained three generative deep learning models (Variational Autoencoder, Conditional GAN, Recurrent Conditional GAN) to synthesize 30,000 artificial land-use trajectories at minimal computational cost across High-LES, Mid-LES, and Low-LES pathway classes, reproducing key variable distributions and passing internal land-balance checks. The Variational Autoencoder framework delivered superior classification performance, showing that deep learning can augment conventional land-system assessment while maintaining consistency with traditional IAM frameworks.

Efficiency divergence in agricultural innovation-to-ecological security transformation: Network analysis of Chinese provincial spillovers

Nihal Ahmed, Józef Ober, Jianjun Huai

Environmental and Sustainability Indicators

DOI: 10.1016/j.indic.2026.101224

View Article
Abstract

Agricultural innovation is a critical pathway for achieving land ecological security under intensifying environmental pressures, yet how it drives outcomes across spatial scales and through network mechanisms remains underexplored. We analyze how Chinese provinces transform agricultural innovation resources into land ecological security outcomes, integrating Slacks-Based Measure models with undesirable outputs and social network analysis, using a modified gravity model to construct interprovincial association networks. Provincial efficiency showed substantial spatial heterogeneity and growing divergence rather than convergence, while the association network maintained constant density but declining functional performance. Network analysis revealed that agricultural innovation differences and geographic distance were the strongest drivers of network formation, and block modelling identified four functionally distinct provincial clusters with hierarchical spillover patterns, showing that complementarity rather than similarity drives effective network formation in sustainability transitions.
