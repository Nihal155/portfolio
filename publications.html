Belowground Ecosystem Multifunctionality Embodied in China's Agricultural Imports: Thermal Geography, Equity Trajectory, and a Multi-Indicator Accounting Framework

N. Ahmed, N.A. Khan, J. Ober, P. Sakiewicz, J. Huai

Ecological Indicators

IF 8.7 Under Review
Abstract

Belowground ecosystem multifunctionality (BEMF), a composite indicator of soil biological and biogeochemical functioning, underpins agricultural productivity yet is absent from consumption-based environmental indicator frameworks. We develop and apply the first annual trade-embodied BEMF indicator system for a national economy, applied to China's agricultural sector (2009–2022), built from a spatially resolved country–sector intensity matrix spanning 2,222 cells across 133 nations (93.9% direct pixel-level data), linked to bilateral trade flows from 61 partners. The framework integrates six components: consumption-based accounting, structural decomposition, thermal stress stratification, biocapacity overdraft, planetary equity boundary tracking, and counterfactual analysis. China's consumption-based BEMF expanded 48.2% while import-embodied BEMF grew 161.7%, with import-volume growth contributing 124% of cumulative change and geographic re-sourcing producing no detectable ecological signal over fourteen years. A thermal stress concentration indicator benchmarked against the 16.4°C suppression threshold shows thermally stressed origins supplied 64.1% of import-embodied BEMF by 2022, with a persistently sub-unity concentration ratio confirming systematic per-ton underperformance of thermally degraded suppliers. A planetary equity boundary exceedance ratio rising from 0.419 to 0.676 projects transgression of the global equal-rights BEMF allocation by approximately 2036 under the full trend, with shorter recent windows placing this later, within 2036–2049. Counterfactual indicators quantify a thermal quality penalty of up to 7.7% of annual consumption-based BEMF, achievable through sourcing geography alone without altering trade volumes or commodity composition. The framework is replicable across any agricultural trading economy, offering an operational tool for integrating belowground indicators into biodiversity monitoring aligned with the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework and SDG 15.3.

Agricultural Trade Externalizes Belowground Ecosystem Loss to Climate-Stressed Exporting Nations

N. Ahmed, N.A. Khan, J. Ober, J. Huai

International Journal of Sustainable Development & World Ecology

IF 6.8 10.1080/13504509.2026.2726387
View Paper
Abstract

International agricultural trade redistributes environmental burdens across borders, yet one cost remains unquantified: the loss of belowground ecosystem multifunctionality (BEMF) in producing nations, and the degree to which climate warming amplifies it. We provide the first global quantification of trade-embodied BEMF loss, coupling 15 soil biological and biogeochemical indicators with the FABIO multi-regional input-output framework (155 matched exporting nations, 123 commodity sectors) and CMIP6 projections under three emissions scenarios to 2100. International trade embodies 14.7% of global cropland BEMF production. Thermally stressed exporters (mean annual temperature above 16.4°C) sustain 37% lower per-hectare BEMF than climatically stable exporters, yet direct a larger share of their production into trade (18.1% vs 10.8%), so supply chains draw disproportionately on the most ecologically depleted regions. Export-driven hotspots concentrate in Brazil, Sudan, India, and Eswatini, with the Brazil-to-China soybean corridor the single largest transfer. Projected global BEMF declines range from 6.2% under low emissions to 14.5% under high emissions by 2100, with the steepest zonal loss (23.8%) in currently cooler regions sitting on the steep portion of the temperature–BEMF curve. The ecological cost of an agricultural import therefore depends not only on what or how much is traded, but on where and under what thermal conditions it is grown. We argue for integrating belowground indicators into consumption-based accounting and applying a Climate Exacerbation Factor within climate-adjusted supply chain governance aligned with the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework.

Deep Learning Approaches for Synthesizing Land Ecological Security Pathways in Global Land-Use Scenarios

N. Ahmed, N.A. Khan, J. Ober, M.F. Shahzad

Resources, Environment and Sustainability

IF 9.2 CiteScore 12.5 10.1016/j.resenv.2026.100345
View Paper
Abstract

Land-use scenario projections are essential tools for sustainable development planning. However, conventional integrated assessment model (IAM) approaches face challenges including computational constraints and limited scenario diversity across land degradation risk categories. We present a deep-learning methodology for categorizing and synthesizing Land Ecological Security (LES) trajectories using artificial scenario generation based on SDG 15.3.1 degradation indicators. Through examination of 1,202 land-use projections from the CMIP6/ScenarioMIP LUH2 database, we establish critical determinants of land-system evolution, particularly forest conversion dynamics and agricultural intensification processes that characterize distinct sustainability pathways. Subsequently, we trained three generative deep learning models (Variational Autoencoder, Conditional GAN, Recurrent Conditional GAN) to synthesize 30,000 artificial land-use trajectories with minimal computational cost across three pathway classifications: High-LES, Mid-LES, and Low-LES categories, reproducing key variable distributions and cross-variable relationships and passing internal land-balance checks for accounting consistency. Our evaluation demonstrates that the Variational Autoencoder framework delivers superior classification performance. This investigation reveals the capacity of deep learning to augment conventional land-system assessment methodologies, enhancing the accessibility and comprehensiveness of land-system scenario analysis through rapid exploration of policy-relevant pathway alternatives while maintaining consistency with traditional IAM frameworks.

Efficiency Divergence in Agricultural Innovation-to-Ecological Security Transformation: Network Analysis of Chinese Provincial Spillovers

N. Ahmed, J. Ober, J. Huai

Environmental and Sustainability Indicators

IF 5.5 CiteScore 5.9 10.1016/j.indic.2026.101224
View Paper
Abstract

Agricultural innovation represents a critical pathway for achieving land ecological security under intensifying environmental pressures, yet how agricultural innovation drives land ecological security across spatial scales and through network mechanisms remains underexplored. We address this gap by analyzing how Chinese provinces transform agricultural innovation resources into land ecological security outcomes while accounting for spatial interdependencies and network effects, testing competing hypotheses from convergence versus cumulative advantage theory and homophily versus complementarity theory. We integrated Slacks-Based Measure models with undesirable outputs and social network analysis, employing a modified gravity model to construct interprovincial association networks. Provincial efficiency demonstrated substantial spatial heterogeneity, while the coefficient of variation increased from 0.487 to 0.629, indicating growing divergence rather than convergence. The association network maintained constant density but declining functional performance, suggesting structural stability amid deteriorating knowledge transfer mechanisms. Multiple Regression Quadratic Assignment Procedure analysis revealed that agricultural innovation differences and geographic distance exerted the strongest positive and negative influences on network formation respectively, challenging assumptions that similarity drives collaboration. Block model analysis identified four functionally distinct provincial clusters, the Broker Board, Net Spillover Segment, Two-way Spillover Panel, and Net Benefit Segment, revealing hierarchical spillover patterns where peripheral provinces depend heavily on external connections for ecological improvements. These findings demonstrate that complementarity rather than similarity drives effective network formation in sustainability transitions, and that achieving land ecological security requires differentiated regional strategies that build peripheral provinces' capacity for autonomous innovation.

Quantifying China's Ecological Assets: A Carbon-Based Assessment of Climate Change and Land Use Change Effects Using Generalized Additive Models

N. Ahmed, N.A. Khan, J. Ober, J. Huai

Sustainable Futures

IF 6.7 CiteScore 3.5 10.1016/j.sftr.2025.101350
View Paper
Abstract

Ecological assets provide vital ecosystem services that sustain human well-being, yet face severe threats from climate change and land use change (CC-LUC). This study evaluates ecological assets in China and quantifies the relative and synergistic effects of CC-LUC using a novel approach based on carbon asset valuation, employing total organic carbon pool per unit area as an ecosystem service value equivalent factor. China's ecological assets in 2021 totaled $164 trillion, with cropland ($1.10 trillion), forest ($7.35 trillion), grassland ($153.17 trillion), wetland ($0.04 trillion), desert ($2.08 trillion), and water bodies ($0.30 trillion) showing significant regional variations from 2001–2021. Climate change predominantly drove cropland value changes in Northern China and wetland changes in Western China, while land use changes more significantly impacted croplands and forests in Western China. Generalized Additive Models reveal complex synergistic CC-LUC effects across land types: cropland, forests, and grasslands exhibited initial growth followed by diminishing returns, while wetlands, deserts, and water bodies showed initial decline at lower CC-LUC levels followed by improvements at higher levels, indicating potential resilience beyond certain thresholds. The findings underscore the necessity for tailored conservation strategies, highlighting the efficacy of sustainable practices in some regions and the urgency for targeted interventions in others.

Social Capital, Crop Differences, and Farmers' Climate Change Adaptation Behaviors: Evidence from Yellow River, China

Z. Chang, N. Ahmed, R. Li, J. Huai

Agriculture

IF 4.5 CiteScore 7.8 10.3390/agriculture15131399
View Paper
Abstract

Against the backdrop of global climate change, enhancing farmers' adaptive capacity to reduce crop production risks has emerged as a critical concern for governments and researchers worldwide. Drawing on social capital theory, this study develops a four-dimensional measurement framework comprising social networks, social trust, social norms, and social participation, using survey data from 1,772 households in the Yellow River Basin. We employ factor analysis to construct comprehensive social capital scores and apply ordered Probit models to examine how social capital influences farmers' climate adaptation behaviors, with particular attention to the moderating roles of agricultural extension interaction and digital literacy. Adoption of climate adaptation behaviors remains low overall (60%), with technical adaptation measures showing particularly poor uptake (13%). Social capital significantly promotes adaptation behaviors, with social trust, networks, and participation demonstrating positive effects, while social norms show no significant impact. Impact mechanisms differ by crop type, with grain producers relying more heavily on social networks and cash crop producers depending more on social trust, and both agricultural extension interaction and digital literacy moderate these relationships, with digital literacy consistently weakening social capital's promotional effects. The findings point to policy recommendations centered on strengthened rural social organization, differentiated agricultural extension systems, and enhanced rural digital infrastructure investment.

The Role of Carbon Peaking Targets in Urban Green Economic Transition: Insights from US City-Level Climate Action Plans

N. Ahmed, N.A. Khan, J. Ober

Global Transitions

IF 4.2 CiteScore 5.1 10.1016/j.glt.2025.01.002
View Paper
Abstract

Carbon peak targets play a crucial role in the global effort to mitigate climate change, with profound implications for green and sustainable economic growth. This research explores the dynamics between carbon peak target policies and urban low-carbon development using a novel delegation-agency framework between central and local governments. Analyzing data from U.S. climate action plans spanning 2010–2020, we employ an advanced difference-in-differences approach to elucidate the causal impact of carbon peak targets on low-carbon transitions. Our findings demonstrate a significant positive correlation between the adoption of these targets and accelerated low-carbon development, with effects modulated by local economic structures, resource availability, and governance transitions, validated through placebo experiments, policy interference controls, and heterogeneity bias assessments. Further investigation reveals that carbon peak targets primarily function through two key mechanisms: stimulating eco-innovation and catalyzing green infrastructure investments, with an inverse relationship between target timeline stringency and low-carbon transition effectiveness. The study offers a unified theoretical framework for analyzing local government constraints and carbon peak targets, and suggests policy prescriptions including recalibrating municipal performance metrics, flexible target frameworks, and fostering synergies between environmental policy and innovation ecosystems.

Synergistic Evaluation of Energy Security and Environmental Sustainability in BRICS Geo-Political Entities: An Integrated Index Framework

N. Ahmed, F. Ore Areche, J.M. More López, V.G. Sánchez Araujo, J.A. Legua Cárdenas, J. Ober

Equilibrium. Quarterly Journal of Economics and Economic Policy

CiteScore 6.6 10.24136/eq.3088
View Paper

Published under the author name "Ahmed Nihal".

Abstract

The increasing demand for energy, driven by economic growth and population expansion, is a critical driver of societal progress. However, the predominant reliance on fossil fuels to meet this demand presents significant challenges, particularly in the rapidly developing BRICS nations (Brazil, Russia, India, China, and South Africa), which face a complex interplay of energy security and environmental sustainability issues. This study develops a tailored Energy Security and Environmental Sustainability Index (ESESI) for BRICS economies to evaluate the robustness of their energy systems and the viability of their ecological practices. The construction of the composite ESESI involves a Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis framework combined with the Weighted Product Method, with optimal and least favorable indicator weights determined through a Multiplicative Data Envelopment Analysis model. The ESESI analysis reveals disparities in the progress made by BRICS nations in enhancing energy security, promoting renewable energy deployment, and mitigating environmental impacts: while some countries demonstrate substantial advancements, others face challenges in improving energy efficiency and reducing carbon emissions. The study underscores the necessity for tailored policies and targeted infrastructural enhancements that align with the unique challenges and strengths of each nation, identifying cross-border investment and advanced energy trade mechanisms as crucial strategies for ensuring environmental sustainability and long-term energy security in the BRICS region.

Natural Disasters and Energy Innovation: Unveiling the Linkage from an Environmental Sustainability Perspective

N. Ahmed, F. Ore Areche, E.R. Saenz Arenas, R.F. Cosio Borda, J.L. Javier-Vidalón, S. Silvera-Arcos, J. Ober, A. Kochmańska

Frontiers in Energy Research

IF 2.4 CiteScore 6.3 10.3389/fenrg.2023.1256219
View Paper
Abstract

Natural disasters, characterized by their escalating frequency and intensification under climate change, underscore the urgent need to reassess our built environments and revise infrastructure design to withstand extreme events. This study explores how natural disasters affect the course of energy technology evolution, using panel data from 1990–2020 and econometric analyses to model the effect of disasters on energy technology advancement across BRICS countries. The findings suggest that natural disasters exert a markedly detrimental effect on energy technology innovation, an influence that is not confined to the year of the disaster but persists for the subsequent four-year period. The adverse impacts of such environmental shocks on energy innovation are mitigated by factors including robust economic growth, government stability, interpersonal globalization, financial openness, trade openness, and social globalization, while epidemics have the most pronounced negative implications for energy innovation. The study identifies a significant inverse relationship between natural disasters and low-carbon energy innovation specifically, with no discernible correlation for non-low-carbon energy innovation, and offers a reference for policymakers seeking to strengthen energy innovation while managing disaster risk and environmental sustainability.

Environmental Regulation, Fiscal Decentralization, and Agricultural Carbon Intensity: A Challenge to Ecological Sustainability Policies in the United States

N. Ahmed, Z. Hamid, K. Ur Rehman, P. Senkus, N.A. Khan, J. Ober

Sustainability

IF 4.1 CiteScore 8.9 10.3390/su15065145
View Paper
Abstract

Investigating the effect of fiscal decentralization on the carbon intensity of agricultural production may help the United States reach its carbon peak and achieve carbon neutrality. This paper examines the spatiotemporal patterns and internal relationships between fiscal decentralization, agricultural carbon intensity, and environmental regulation, using a spatial Durbin model on panel data for 49 U.S. states from 2000 to 2019. The study finds that environmental regulations play a significant role in reducing regional agricultural carbon emissions. Fiscal decentralization, which grants local governments more financial autonomy, has a positive but insignificant impact on carbon emissions, indicating that local governments tend to prioritize economic development over environmental protection, and does not substantially moderate the relationship between environmental regulation and carbon emissions. To promote low-carbon agriculture and ensure coordinated economic and environmental development, the study recommends optimizing the fiscal decentralization system, formulating region-specific policies, and strengthening oversight of local government competencies, while noting that city- or county-level data would sharpen understanding of these relationships going forward.

Intensifying Effects of Climate Change in Food Loss: A Threat to Food Security in Turkey

N. Ahmed, F. Ore Areche, G.G. Cotrina Cabello, P.D. Córdova Trujillo

Sustainability

IF 4.1 CiteScore 8.9 10.3390/su15010350
View Paper
Abstract

Turkey faces growing concern over the effects of climate change, weather unpredictability, and severe events on agricultural production, food loss, and livelihoods. This study assesses the risk climate change poses to Turkey from the perspective of food grain loss and food security, using data on exposure to extreme climate events from 1991 to 2019. The paper contributes theoretically by identifying the relationship between food waste, food imports, food prices, and economic growth, and empirically by analyzing the impact of food grain loss on these variables, deriving policy implications using a vector auto-regression (VAR) model and derivative analysis. Food grain loss is found to correlate negatively with food security, as it increases reliance on food imports, and losses in food supply contribute significantly to price increases, while GDP growth was a comparatively weak driver. The findings indicate that climate change threatens food security and hampers Turkey's progress toward sustainable development goals, particularly no poverty and zero hunger, harming both food security and the broader economy.

Technical Efficiency Analysis of Layer and Broiler Poultry Farmers in Pakistan

N.A. Khan, M. Ali, N. Ahmad, M.A. Abid, S. Kusch-Brandt

Agriculture

IF 4.5 CiteScore 7.8 10.3390/agriculture12101742
View Paper

Nihal Ahmed published under the author name "N. Ahmad".

Abstract

Achieving high production with limited resources is a major challenge for poultry farmers in developing economies such as Pakistan, making improvements in technical efficiency (TE) a promising strategy. This study analyzes TE across a representative sample of 210 poultry farms in Punjab, Pakistan, comprising 105 layer farms (battery cage, egg production) and 105 broiler farms (environmental control shed, meat production), using a Cobb–Douglas stochastic frontier production approach with an inefficiency effect model to estimate TE levels and identify the factors that influence efficiency. Flock size, labor, feed, and water consumption were positively related to egg production, while vaccination was insignificant; for broiler operations, flock size, feed, and water consumption were positively related to output, while labor and vaccination were insignificant. Farmer age, education, experience, access to credit, and access to extension services all had a significant, positive influence on technical efficiency for both layer and broiler farmers. Mean TE was estimated at 89% for layer farmers and 92% for broiler farmers relative to the efficient production frontier, implying that egg production could rise by 11% and meat production by 8% through more efficient use of existing resources and technology.

The Role of Educating the Labor Force in Sustaining a Green Economy in MINT Countries: Panel Symmetric and Asymmetric Approach

N. Ahmed, A.A. Sheikh, B. Hassan, S.N. Khan, R. Córdova Borda, J.M.C. Huamán

Sustainability

IF 4.1 CiteScore 8.9 10.3390/su141912067
View Paper
Abstract

The economies of Mexico, Indonesia, Nigeria, and Turkey (the MINT countries) have achieved significant economic growth over the years, but without protecting the quality of their environments at the same time, casting a shadow over the sustainability of their continued development. A more educated workforce may boost an economy's absorption capacity and enhance the efficiency of green technology, both of which can contribute to lower greenhouse gas emissions. This study examines the relationship between educating the labor force and environmental sustainability in the MINT economies using panel ARDL-PMG and NARDL-PMG techniques on data spanning 1995–2020. The ARDL-PMG results show that a more highly educated workforce plays a vital role in mitigating CO2 emissions, while the NARDL-PMG results show that positive shifts in workforce education strongly reduce CO2 emissions in the short run, whereas negative shifts have a positive effect on emissions over the long run. The study recommends that MINT authorities boost education and training for their workforces as a means of keeping CO2 emissions down.

Nexus Between Nuclear Energy Consumption and Carbon Footprint in Asia Pacific Region: Policy Toward Environmental Sustainability

N. Ahmed, F. Mahboob, Z. Hamid, A.A. Sheikh, M.S. Ali, W. Glabiszewski, A. Wysokińska-Senkus, P. Senkus, S. Cyfert

Energies

IF 3.9 CiteScore 8.3 10.3390/en15196956
View Paper
Abstract

Equitable distribution of energy sources is regarded as central to many of the United Nations' Sustainable Development Goals, and nations must balance the three dimensions of the global energy trilemma: energy affordability, energy access, and ecological balance. This study investigates the impact of nuclear energy, technological advancement, renewable energy, non-renewable energy, and natural resources on carbon footprints in the five largest nuclear-energy-consuming countries of the Asia Pacific region, China, India, Japan, Pakistan, and South Korea, using contemporary second-generation panel econometric methods that confirm long-run cointegration between the series. The results show that nuclear and renewable energy use significantly improve environmental quality, while technological advancement and non-renewable energy use considerably reduce environmental sustainability, and natural resources play a negative role over the long term. Causality running from nuclear power development is found to be unidirectional, while bidirectional causality exists between technological innovation, renewable energy, non-renewable energy, and carbon footprints. The study recommends that these countries combine energy policy actions and build consistent energy strategies by harmonizing the essential features of global nuclear energy toward a well-calibrated energy structure.

Nexus Between Cyclical Innovation in Green Technologies and CO2 Emissions in Nordic Countries: Consent Toward Environmental Sustainability

N. Ahmed, F. Ore Areche, D.D. Del Carpio Nieto, R.F. Cosio Borda, B. Cansino Gonzales, P. Senkus, P. Siemiński, A. Skrzypek

Sustainability

IF 4.1 CiteScore 8.9 10.3390/su141811768
View Paper
Abstract

Environmental degradation poses a serious threat to sustainable global production and consumption, and policymakers broadly agree that the growing use and production of carbon-intensive technologies has intensified the harms of carbon dioxide emissions. In response, many nations have enacted stringent regulations and encouraged green technology innovation across corporate and government organizations, yet the non-linear influence of shocks in green-technology research, development, and innovation on CO2 emissions in the Nordic countries has been little studied. Using panel data from 1995 to 2019, this research explores the asymmetric relationship between green technology innovation and CO2 emissions, validating cointegration between the variables through the Westerlund and Johansen–Fisher panel cointegration tests. The CS-ARDL results show that negative shocks to green technology development contribute to carbon dioxide emissions during recessions, while green technology innovation can reduce emissions during periods of economic expansion; GDP growth increases CO2 emissions while renewable energy use decreases them. In summary, advances in environmentally friendly technology and levels of carbon dioxide emissions are found to move in inverse proportion to one another.

Causal Linkage Among Agricultural Insurance, Air Pollution, and Agricultural Green Total Factor Productivity in United States: Pairwise Granger Causality Approach

N. Ahmed, Z. Hamid, F. Mahboob, K. Ur Rehman, M.S. Ali, P. Senkus, J. Ober

Agriculture

IF 4.5 CiteScore 7.8 10.3390/agriculture12091320
View Paper
Abstract

Agricultural insurance and green agriculture are closely related: agricultural insurance not only motivates farmers to adopt environmentally friendly production technology and improves production effectiveness, it also helps reduce the volume of chemicals released into the environment. This study investigates the dynamic relationship between agricultural insurance, air pollution, and agricultural green total factor productivity using the panel autoregressive distributed lags (PMG) method on data from 50 U.S. states between 2005 and 2019. The results show considerable co-integration and cross-sectional dependence between the three variables: expanding agricultural insurance can boost green total factor output but may also exacerbate air pollution, while significant air pollution does not itself increase the green total factor productivity of agricultural production. A panel Granger causality test reveals a one-way causal relationship running from agricultural insurance and air pollution to green total factor productivity, and from air pollution to agricultural green total factor productivity. The study concludes that improving agricultural insurance coverage or reducing air pollution can boost agricultural green total factor output, with long-term implications for agricultural policy and environmental management.

Energy Diversification: A Friend or Foe to Economic Growth in Nordic Countries? A Novel Energy Diversification Approach

N. Ahmed, A.A. Sheikh, F. Mahboob, M.S. Ali, E. Jasińska, M. Jasiński

Energies

IF 3.9 CiteScore 8.3 10.3390/en15155422
View Paper
Abstract

Energy is essential to economic growth, yet energy production emits carbon dioxide, a primary driver of environmental deterioration and climate change; diversifying energy sources can help reconcile these two goals. This study investigates the relationship between energy diversification and economic growth in the Nordic countries using a novel energy diversification measure and a Nonlinear Panel Autoregressive Distributed Lag (NPARDL) approach applied to data from 1998 to 2018. The results show that these nations experience favorable economic growth when the long-term diversity of their energy sources increases, but see negative economic effects from energy diversification in the near term. These findings suggest that energy diversification benefits Nordic economic growth over the long run, though further research is needed to establish whether the same holds for developing economies, and that complementary preventative policy measures should accompany continued energy diversification.

Exploring the Causal Relationship Among Green Taxes, Energy Intensity, and Energy Consumption in Nordic Countries: Dumitrescu and Hurlin Causality Approach

N. Ahmed, A.A. Sheikh, Z. Hamid, P. Senkus, R. Córdova Borda

Energies

IF 3.9 CiteScore 8.3 10.3390/en15145199
View Paper
Abstract

Improving energy efficiency and reducing environmental harm through environmental laws and green taxes are widely regarded as central drivers of climate change policy. This analysis clarifies the role green taxes play in lowering energy use and energy intensity from 1994 to 2020, examining how green taxes interact with energy intensity and consumption across four Nordic countries. Using fully modified ordinary least squares (FMOLS), panel dynamic ordinary least squares (PDOLS), and panel quantile regression, the results show that green taxes help reduce total energy consumption and increase energy efficiency by motivating governments, companies, and citizens to pursue innovation in environment-related technology. The study argues that regulations ensuring the displacement of non-renewable resources while increasing energy efficiency should be a priority for building a more sustainable energy system.

Green Finance and Green Energy Nexus in ASEAN Countries: A Bootstrap Panel Causality Test

N. Ahmed, F. Ore Areche, A.A. Sheikh, A. Lahiani

Energies

IF 3.9 CiteScore 8.3 10.3390/en15145068
View Paper
Abstract

Green energy is a crucial component of meeting rising energy demand and addressing climate change, though the potential drawbacks of these technologies are often overlooked; scaling up green energy depends on financing investment that delivers environmental benefits through instruments such as green banks, green bonds, community-based green funds, and green central banking. Using a STIRPAT model approach and annual data spanning 2002–2018 for six ASEAN member countries, this study investigates the relationship between CO2 emissions, green finance, energy efficiency, and a green energy index, applying the Westerlund and Johansen–Fisher tests for long-term equilibrium and the generalized method of moments (GMM) to estimate Granger causal links between variables. The results indicate that green bonds are an effective mechanism for promoting green energy projects and considerably reducing CO2 emissions, suggesting that governments should establish long-term supporting policies to increase private investment in green energy projects and ensure sustainable growth while addressing environmental challenges.

Assessing the Impact of Policy Measures in Reducing the COVID-19 Pandemic: A Case Study of South Asia

D. Khan, N. Ahmed, B. Mehmed, I. Haq

Sustainability

IF 4.1 CiteScore 8.9 10.3390/su132011315
View Paper
Abstract

The COVID-19 pandemic is considered one of the world's most urgent health disasters and among the greatest challenges humanity has faced since World War II, raising pressing questions about the effectiveness of the extraordinary measures undertaken worldwide to curb the spread of the coronavirus. This study applies the Westerlund cointegration test and a Pooled Mean Group–Autoregressive Distributed Lag (PMG-ARDL) model to investigate the effectiveness of government policy measures in reducing the COVID-19 pandemic, using weekly South Asian data from 1 January 2020 to 31 May 2021. The results confirm a cointegration relationship between government policy measures and the pandemic's trajectory, and show that economic support, stringency, and health and containment measures each played a significant role in reducing the spread of COVID-19. The study concludes that both pharmaceutical and non-pharmaceutical measures substantially mitigated the pandemic in South Asia, and recommends government financial support for vulnerable families, continued stringency and containment measures, free vaccine access for developing countries, and stimulus support for health research as key policy priorities.
